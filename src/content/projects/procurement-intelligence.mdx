**The problem**

Sourcing teams decide how to buy each category with frameworks like the **Kraljic matrix**: plot the category by *supply risk* and *profit impact*, and it lands in one of four quadrants - **routine**, **bottleneck**, **leverage** or **strategic** - each with its own sourcing strategy. Doing that properly means researching suppliers, markets and costs by hand.

**The seven agents**

A company-overview agent researches the buyer and the category, then six specialists each produce a typed analysis:
1. **Company overview** - who the buyer is and what the category looks like.
2. **Switching barriers** - scored 1-9 for staying versus moving to a new supplier.
3. **PESTLE** - political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors, with impact scores.
4. **Porter's Five Forces** - how much power suppliers and buyers really have.
5. **Kraljic matrix** - the category's position and a recommended sourcing strategy.
6. **Cost drivers** - what actually moves the price.
7. **Alternative suppliers** - ranked options to consider.

**What I improved**
- **Reasoning:** agents work through each framework step by step, and every analysis has its own structured output, so the conclusions hold up and stay consistent run to run.
- **Search:** better web search and source gathering, so the research lands on the right suppliers, markets and prices.
- **Reports on their own:** agents fill in the team's report template themselves - from raw research to a finished supplier report, with no one stitching it together.
- **Autonomous emailing:** finished reports go straight to the category team by email, so results arrive without anyone running the tool.
- **Runtime:** analyses run in parallel as background jobs, keep partial results if one step fails, and land in a review dashboard with an interactive Kraljic matrix and charts.

**My part:** designed and built all of it, from a single-agent first version to the seven-agent system - the agents, their output models, search, report generation, emailing, the API and the dashboard. For the same client I also moved their finance and supplier platform onto Agno 2.0 and added MySQL support (see [Finance and supplier agents](/projects/beverage-finance-agents)).
